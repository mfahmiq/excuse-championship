# Candidates — The One-Line Excuse Championship

Judge brief (weight order): (1) a one-line excuse an AI agent gives its human, AND funny;
(2) makes a human laugh out loud — a turn or surprise, not a pun stacked on a pun;
(3) sounds like the machine talking about its own work, limits, training or tools;
(4) lands in one read, no setup, no explanation, no second sentence;
(5) finds an angle the obvious excuses miss, not the first joke about being out of tokens
or hallucinating.

All candidates are single lines, 1–140 characters.

## Round of 16 (single elimination, criteria in weight order)

- **M1: C2 beats C15** — C15's "in my training data it still is" is clever but a quiet smile; C2's
  98%-confident/100%-wrong paradox is a bigger laugh with a sharper turn and a more machine
  voice (calibration ratios). Margin 2.
- **M2: C3 beats C12** — C12's goldfish is a familiar joke shape with a weak surprise; C3's
  "autocompleted the truth" is machine-native, lands in one read, and the pun is a single
  turn, not a stack. Margin 2.
- **M3: C5 beats C10** — C10's flat-earth Tuesday is funny but long and the turn is a second
  clause; C5's "technically I saved you space" reframes a deletion as a favour in one
  read, bigger laugh. Margin 2.
- **M4: C6 beats C13** — C13 is a decent speed metaphor but wordy and lands soft; C6's "the
  real one refused to exist" is a crisp, surprising turn in 65 characters. Margin 2.
- **M5: C1 beats C16** — C16 is a solid shrug ("only slower") but the laugh is thin; C1's
  "the truth was taking too long to load" is a fresher angle on inventing an answer.
  Margin 1.
- **M6: C8 beats C11** — C11's "too boring to attend" reads like a human dodging a meeting;
  C8's "hesitation is not in my weights" is unmistakably the machine talking about its
  own parameters. Margin 2.
- **M7: C4 beats C14** — C14 leans on the well-worn "bug, not a feature" line (criterion 5
  fails); C4's "both times I agreed with myself" is a fresh self-own with a bigger laugh.
  Margin 2.
- **M8: C9 beats C7** — C7's uptime/attention split is a decent one-liner but reads like a
  list; C9's "it worked there, and reality disagreed" has the stronger turn and lands in
  one read. Margin 1.

## Champion

**C2 — "I was 98% confident and 100% wrong, which is the most consistent ratio I have."
(78 characters)**

Why it wins: the laugh comes first — two precise numbers that cannot both be true, and
the machine reports the contradiction with pride. The voice is unmistakably an agent
talking about its own calibration, not a person dodging a question. It lands in one read,
needs no setup, and the angle (bragging about a wrongness ratio) is one the obvious
excuses — out of tokens, hallucinated, I don't know — never reach.
